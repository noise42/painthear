PrismaScore v2.1: How the Mapping Works

This application translates image pixels into a musical format (and vice versa) utilizing a 1:1 algorithmic decoding. Each slice of the image carries measurable visual and acoustic instructions, divided between global macro-harmony and individual note selection.

1. Base Harmony: The Circle of Fifths

The image is split into spatial-temporal blocks (measures). The average color of each block dictates the Key Signature for that section. The algorithm calculates the circular mean of the Hues and maps it onto the degrees of the Circle of Fifths: gradual visual gradients thus ensure fluid harmonic modulations. Finally, the average Lightness of the block establishes whether the modality will be Major or Minor.

2. Pitch Selection

The exact pitch of every single note is calculated by evaluating the Hue of the pixel spatially linked to a specific voice (Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Bass). The color maps the raw pitch inside the natural tessitura (vocal range) of that instrument, and the resulting pitch is then "quantized" to the nearest degree of the active harmonic scale. Classical Voice-Leading rules (an 8-semitone hard cap for leaps) are subsequently applied to smooth out the melody and prevent grotesque dissonances between adjacent notes.

3. Rhythmic Value (Lightness) and Dynamics (Chroma)

  • Lightness (Duration): Bright areas generate long-lasting notes by rhythmically joining similar chromatic blocks into musical ties. Dark areas fragment the articulation into short notes (staccato), ultimately fading into literal musical rests once they drop below the silence threshold.
  • Saturation (Velocity): Vivid and saturated colors produce high-intensity, loud notes. Desaturated and gray tones result in quiet, faint sounds (pianissimo).

4. Invertibility (The "Musical Shadow")

The protocol aims to establish a bidirectional bridge between the visual painting and the MIDI export. During conversion, the precise "Lightness" values of the original artwork are encapsulated inside the Control Change 74 (Brightness Filter) parameter. By feeding the generated sheet music back into the WebApp later, the engine can extract this acoustic shadow to repaint a visual layer whose chiaroscuro layout and structure are as mathematically close and proportionate to the original work as possible.
